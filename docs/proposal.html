2  Proposal

A research proposal is one of the most important parts of a research project. It formulates a theoretical background and a specific plan you aim to follow.

Why do I need a research proposal?

Every project, academic or not, needs a plan. A research proposal is essentially a formalized, written plan of your future steps.

When doing academic research, there are often many roads you can take - or many ways a project can go. This is true for any project, too. For example, if you want to paint a wall, you can choose between many colors! However, some of these options are more logical than others. Maybe you do not like some of the colors, maybe some colors are not available, maybe some colors clash with the furniture you own… In order to move forward with your project you have to make some decisions (i.e., choose a color), and you have to be able to explain this decision (at least to yourself).

A research proposal helps you think ahead since it asks you to make certain decisions prior to starting. By completing this step, you will save your future self a lot of time.

How do I write a research proposal?

  1. Read the literature previously shared with you.

  2. Find a set of variables you are genuinely interested in exploring.

  3. Think about the measures you can use to research these variables. Refer to the planned questionnaires and scales.

  4. Do not plan to mix questionnaires into one variable, and do not split them up unless you have a good reason (or a published paper provided a good argument for it).

  5. Formulate a research question and hypotheses with this set of variables.

  6. Write all the plans up, together with all other parts of the proposal.

In practice, it takes a few tries to complete the step 5. It is likely that you will have to go through the steps 2-5 multiple times, each time reading more literature in order to improve your research question. This is normal and part of the process.

What do I include in a research proposal?

Please only follow the below guidelines when writing your proposal. The word count is an estimate, and you are welcome to go a bit over if needed. However, try to be as concise as possible while not losing important information.

There is no minimum or maximum amount of citations you should include. However, it is unlikely for the proposal to be written according to the guidelines without a substantial amount of citations.

Scientific background (~200 words)

Briefly describe the current state of the literature that led to your project idea. Provide definitions/descriptions of relevant concepts. Give more attention to a few key studies that directly address your research question.

Objectives & implications (~200 words)

Clearly describe the aims of your project. Describe how your research complements the current literature, or how it fits a gap. Argue why this is important to do. You can argue for real-life relevance, too, not just for scientific benefit. These should all follow from the previously described background. You can include your research question here.

Hypotheses (as many words as needed)

Formulate clear, specific hypotheses based on your research question. You can have multiple hypotheses, but you can also have just one - it is fully dependent on your question. For each hypothesis, state an expectation, explain how this expectation follows from previous literature.

An expectation needs to be specific - it is not enough to state you expect something to be different.

Example of a bad expectation:

I hypothesize I will/expect to find a difference in trust in science between the two groups.

Example of a good expectation:

I hypothesize I will/expect to find lower scores (this specifies the difference) on questionnaires measuring beliefs in science (this specifies the variable and the measure) in men compared to women (this specifies the direction of difference).*

* This would be even better if you state the exact difference you expect. E.g., you expect to find scores that are at least 5 points lower. Remember: be as specific as possible.

Methods (~700 words)

For each section, provide a clear description and motivation for the choice made.

Design

Briefly address the study design (e.g., cross-sectional, longitudinal). State what type of measures will be used (e.g., self-reports) and where data collection will take place (e.g., online).

Participants

Indicate the expected characteristics of the sample, namely mention the sample size and the population you want to sample. The population will most likely be undergraduate students at Tilburg University. However, in case of availability, the population could focus on other groups (e.g., adults, specific genders, etc.). This is to be determined.

Measures

Describe each variable of your study, how it is measured, and what the expected output of the measure will be (e.g., a singular number, a score, a category). For questionnaires, include the name of the questionnaires, scale values (e.g., Likert scale from 1 to 5, with 1 =… , 2 = …), provide examples of questions. Always cite the original creator of the questionnaire. Provide psychometric data of questionnaires if available in previous work.

Statistical analysis

Program

Indicate the program and the version of the program you plan to use for your analyses. You can use any program you want, including SPSS and R. It is likely that all of you will use SPSS, but I can also guide you through analyses in other software.

Power

Execute and report a power analysis. This is an important step at this stage. You can use G*Power software. Provide a screenshot of the output you got.

Analysis

Indicate which statistical tests you will use, and describe the outcomes that would confirm or reject your hypothesis. The statistical test you choose has to be able to answer your hypothesis. If you are in doubt, reach out - or change your research question.

It is possible your research question may need a statistical method you are not yet familiar with. In that case you have to change your research question. Be mindful of the questions that can be answered with your current statistical knowledge. It is always better to have correct but simple analyses instead of incorrect but complicated ones.

How do I format the proposal?

Use the standard formatting: APA-style, Times New Roman, 12-point font, line spacing 2.0. Include page numbers.

Deadline and submission

The deadline for the completed proposal is October 2 at 10:00.

The proposal is submitted through email to me. This is the official way to submit it.

Questions

If you have any questions regarding the choice of variables, your research question, hypotheses, power, or analyses - please reach out.

In this orienting stage of the project, I will not be strict about the emails you send me, and they will not be counted for the official correspondence limit. However, my response times remain the same, so please plan ahead.
